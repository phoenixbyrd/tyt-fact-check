Iran Threatens the One Move Trump CAN’T Survive
Published September 27, 2026 · Watch on YouTube
65Mixed
DeceptiveHonest

The big picture checks out — the US–Iran war is real and ongoing, Hormuz restrictions are in place, and Ghalibaf really did threaten Gulf oil and gas infrastructure. The problem is timing: two events presented as breaking this weekend actually happened weeks ago.

Key non-truthful segments (3)

ModerateStale news presented as breaking
The video presents the US sinking of three Iranian tankers as having happened “over the weekend.” (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

Wire reporting dates the tanker strike to September 5, 2026 — three weeks before the video — not September 26–27. The event happened; presenting it as this weekend's news is materially misleading.

ModerateStale news presented as breaking
The video presents a fresh Iranian missile barrage against a base in Jordan as breaking news. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

The underlying September 8–9 attack on the Jordan base was wire-reported at the time. The video revives it as a September 28 development, which it is not.

MinorUnverified detail
The video cites Iranian cluster-munition use in the Jordan-base strike. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

No independent reporting was found confirming cluster munitions in that strike. Unverified stays unverifiable — noted here so it is not repeated as fact.

U.S. Weapons FALL Into Houthi Hands After Major Defeat
Published September 27, 2026 · Watch on YouTube
90Honest
DeceptiveHonest

Largely solid. Red Sea shipping disruption and Saudi oil infrastructure attacks are wire-supported, Houthi forces really did capture equipment including a tank after Saudi-backed forces withdrew, and Gabbard's testimony is on the record. Two small deductions for overstatement and speculation.

Key non-truthful segments (2)

MinorOverstatement
The video describes Red Sea shipping as broadly shut down. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

Shipping disruption and attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure are real and wire-reported, but the shutdown is not total — not all shipping is equally affected. The video's framing overstates how complete the disruption is.

MinorSpeculation presented as near-certain
The video predicts the war will continue throughout Trump's term. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

No one can verify a prediction about how long the war lasts. As analysis it would be fine; as a near-certain outcome it overreaches. In the video's favor: Gabbard's congressional testimony that US intelligence assessed Iran was not building a nuclear weapon is on the record — though that assessment must be read with its proper scope and date.

Mark Levin CAUGHT In Shady Dark Money Scheme
Published September 27, 2026 · Watch on YouTube
35Misleading
DeceptiveHonest

A mix of genuine financial findings and serious distortions. The 990s really do show Landmark Legal Foundation's assets more than tripling to about $17.4M, zero dollars in grants, and the officer pay figures check out. But two claims are flat wrong and one is framed to mislead — enough to sink the video.

Key non-truthful segments (4)

SevereFalse
The guest claims the IRS “flagged” a $5M 2022 donation to Landmark Legal Foundation as strange dark money. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

There is no evidence of any such IRS notation — the IRS does not publicly flag individual donations on specific returns. This appears to be the guest's embellishment, not a fact.

Sources: ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer — Landmark Legal Foundation 990s
ModerateMisleading
The guest says Landmark spends $2.6M a year on payroll for 20 employees. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

The $2.61M figure is Landmark's total functional expenses for FY2024, not payroll. Actual payroll (officer compensation plus salaries plus payroll taxes) was roughly $913K in FY2022 and about $1.17M in FY2023. Total expenses were relabeled as payroll.

Sources: ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer — Landmark Legal Foundation 990s
ModerateMisleading
The guest says America First Legal received a single $27.1M anonymous donation in one year from the Bradley Fund. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

The Bradley Impact Fund gave about $27.4M across 2021–2022 — not a single $27.1M gift in one year. The dollar magnitude is real; the “one donation” framing is wrong.

MinorUnverified
The guest describes a “like $5M” single donation to Landmark in 2022. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

FY2022 was genuinely a spike year — contributions jumped to $7.79M from $1.66M in 2021 — but Schedule B donor identities are redacted, so a single $5M gift versus several large ones cannot be confirmed.

Sources: ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer — Landmark Legal Foundation 990s
Republicans Quietly Ditch Trump As Midterms Near
Published September 27, 2026 · Watch on YouTube
65Mixed
DeceptiveHonest

The polling numbers at the video's core are real — 32% approval, 73% Republican approval, Democrats up eight on the generic ballot per Reuters/Ipsos. But the video inflates, omits, and speculates around them.

Key non-truthful segments (5)

ModerateInflated
The video says Trump's prior Republican support was “almost 100%, like 98%.” (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

The preceding Reuters result was 82% Republican approval. Describing that as “like 98%” inflates the baseline to make the drop look steeper.

MinorUnverified
The video cites exact Fox News poll figures — 90% on gas/groceries, 80% on healthcare, 77% on housing. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

Those exact combined figures could not be confirmed in the primary poll materials that were opened. The underlying polling trend is real; the precise numbers as stated are not verified.

MinorSelective omission
The video reports calls to investigate Donald Trump Jr.'s wedding funding. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

The calls are real, but the video omits that Rep. Curtis's letter also targeted Hunter Biden, and omits Trump's reimbursement claim — decisive context for judging the story.

MinorUnverifiable
The video relays Tucker Carlson's claim about a private conversation with Trump. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

Tucker Carlson really made the claim, but the underlying private conversation and the alleged Trump quote are uncorroborated. A claim about a private talk with no record cannot be verified.

MinorUnverified motive
The video frames the Iran war as fought “at Israel's behest.” (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

The war itself is wire-confirmed. But attributing its motive to Israel's behest is an assertion about intent with no supporting evidence — motive cannot be inferred.

Dave Smith Puts Ben Shapiro ON BLAST
Published September 27, 2026 · Watch on YouTube
95Honest
DeceptiveHonest

The checkable claims hold up. Shapiro's CBN remarks about Tucker Carlson — that he “traffics in antisemitism as a matter of course, literally every day” — are verified nearly word-for-word from CBN's own upload. The one deduction is for specific old tweets about Ron Paul that could not be located.

Key non-truthful segment (1)

MinorUnverified
The video describes Shapiro's old tweets about Ron Paul — claiming he fantasized about strangling Jews, calling him alt-right and a Nazi. (Paraphrased from auto-generated captions.)

Shapiro's hostility to Ron Paul in 2012 is documented — he called Paul “anti-semitic” and “manifestly insane.” But the specific tweets as described could not be found in any archive, and “alt-right” as a label is anachronistic for that era, suggesting statements from different years were conflated.
