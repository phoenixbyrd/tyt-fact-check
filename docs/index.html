Benjamin Netanyahu Caught BEGGING For Help

DeceptiveHonest

The video reports on Haaretz's claim that Netanyahu secretly flew to the UAE to ask President bin Zayed to publicly deny reports that he was warned before the October 7 attack, and reviews other pre-October 7 warnings from Egypt and the 'Jericho Wall' blueprint. The underlying event is real and corroborated by AP and the Times of Israel, but the video presents the disputed UAE warning as settled fact while omitting official denials, selectively quotes Netanyahu's 2001 remarks on 9/11, and asserts without evidence that he deliberately let the attack happen. Overall verdict: Mixed.

Key non-truthful segments (4)

MinorDeceptive framing / loaded language
Video title: "Benjamin Netanyahu Caught BEGGING For Help" (~0:00)

The underlying event is verified: Haaretz, citing an Israeli and a regional official, reported that Netanyahu's secret UAE trip was to ask President bin Zayed to publicly deny reports of a pre-October 7 warning, and AP confirmed the visit's purpose. But no reporting describes Netanyahu 'begging'; he 'asked.' The title's 'begging' frames a diplomatic request as panicked pleading, which the sources do not support.

Sources: Times of Israel
ModerateOmission
"And it turns out that The New York Times has now confirmed Haaretz reporting. Yes, that's absolutely right." (~0:40)

The New York Times did independently report, citing anonymous sources, that UAE President bin Zayed warned Netanyahu before October 7. But the allegation originates from a book's claim and is officially denied: the UAE Foreign Ministry maintained that no such warning was issued, and Netanyahu's office denied the reports. The video presents the warning as settled fact ('that's absolutely right') without mentioning any denial, omitting material context a viewer needs to judge the claim.

Sources: Times of Israel
ModerateOmission (selective quotation)
"And Netanyahu, he said this is very good for the Israelis. It didn't bother him at all. Didn't bother him a bit." (~15:07)

In the September 12, 2001 New York Times interview, Netanyahu was asked about the effect of 9/11 on U.S.-Israel relations and replied 'It's very good' - but immediately corrected himself: 'Well, not very good, but it will generate immediate sympathy.' The video quotes only the first phrase and asserts the attacks 'didn't bother him at all,' omitting the on-the-record self-correction that changes the meaning of the exchange.

Sources: Wikiquote (citing The New York Times, Sept 12, 2001)
ModerateOpinion presented as fact / motive inference
"It looks like exactly what he planned... he would let the attack happen for his own political purposes... he would do it to anyone to gain political advantage which he thinks is good for Zionism and the Israeli project." (~14:50)

No public evidence or official investigation has shown that Netanyahu deliberately allowed the October 7 attack to happen; the IDF's internal probes, reported by AP, found massive intelligence failures and misconceptions about Hamas, not intent, and Netanyahu denies ignoring warnings. Asserting as a conclusion that he 'planned' it and stating his motive as fact is speculative and goes beyond the evidence.

Key non-truthful segments (3)

The video repeatedly asserts genocide as an established fact rather than a contested characterization, which misleads viewers about the legal status of the claim.

ModerateFalse implication / exaggeration
"They've murdered more journalists in Gaza than all the other conflicts in the world combined." (~5:04)

The Committee to Protect Journalists' September 2026 review attributes 191 Palestinian journalists' deaths to Israeli forces in Gaza and counts 247 war-related journalist and media worker deaths across Gaza, Yemen, Lebanon, Iran and Israel since October 7, 2023. Gaza is the deadliest conflict for journalists CPJ has documented, but CPJ's published data does not support the unqualified claim that Gaza's toll exceeds 'all the other conflicts in the world combined' - CPJ's all-time global total is far higher, and the video omits any time frame that would make the comparison meaningful.

Sources: Committee to Protect Journalists
ModerateFalse claim (hyperbole presented as fact)
"They've destroyed every single building." (~4:47)

Destruction in Gaza is vast, but not literally total: a UNOSAT satellite assessment found around 201,290 buildings affected - roughly 78% of structures per UN-Habitat - with about 134,422 destroyed. Stating that 'every single building' was destroyed is demonstrably false as a factual claim, even as hyperbole, and overstates the documented damage.

Sources: Middle East Monitor (citing UNOSAT)

About & methodology

TYT Fact Check watches every full-length video The Young Turks post to YouTube each day and rates its honesty — not with a checklist of trivia, but by pulling out the key non-truthful segments and explaining what makes each one misleading.

  • The unit is the misleading segment. Selective omission, false implication, cherry-picked data, strawmen, deceptive framing, loaded language that implies something false, or an outright false claim — each gets called out with the fuller, accurate picture.
  • Sliding truthfulness score. Every video scores 0–100. Each segment deducts by severity: Minor −5, Moderate −15, Severe −30. Bands: 90–100 Honest, 70–89 Mostly honest, 50–69 Mixed, 30–49 Misleading, 0–29 Deceptive.
  • Evidence first. Every segment explanation cites named sources (primary documents, official data, wire reporting) you can open yourself.
  • Verify the event, not the echo. A claim is treated as true only when the underlying event is confirmed by direct evidence — not merely because someone said it happened. If the event can't be verified, we say so — but only after a genuine dig: primary sources first (transcripts, filings, dockets, datasets, official statements), at least three independent outlets or wires rather than echoes of one report, official channels (press briefings, agency releases, court dockets, the congressional record, FEC and nonprofit filings), and international or primary-language sources for foreign events. Fact-checkers are leads, never evidence. If a claim is hours old, it is flagged as developing and re-checked before the edition finalizes. Some claims are structurally unverifiable — predictions, private conversations with no record — and after the dig we mark them honestly rather than guessing. This full search is standard for every video, and any claim headed for an unverified finding gets a mandatory second sweep with different search angles before the edition finalizes.
  • Corrections: if new evidence changes a score, the edition is updated and the change is noted. No stealth edits.
  • One edition per day, plus a daily podcast episode walking through each video’s score and misleading segments. Not affiliated with The Young Turks.
